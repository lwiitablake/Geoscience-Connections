Three ways to contribute a connection

  1. Documented connection: a traceable source explicitly establishes the relationship. Record the exact claim supported and what was actually reviewed.
  2. Illustrative application: sources support the underlying Earth-science concept; the particular task is an explicitly proposed application. Do not imply a published implementation.
  3. Exploratory idea: a plausible, unverified proposal that still needs source discovery and disciplinary review. Keep it in the candidate queue until reviewed. No fabricated references or claims of proof.

A repeatable update review

  1. Check existing reference pages for changed claims, new editions, broken links, corrections, and retractions. An unavailable link alone does not invalidate a connection.
  2. Search across all field families over time, prioritizing gaps and current leads. Record search terms, dates, sources checked, and which families were reviewed.
  3. Capture a concrete task, the relevant Earth process or material, and an explanation of the connection. Separate direct evidence from your interpretation.
  4. Compare with existing records to avoid duplicates. Preserve stable connection and source IDs; record proposed edits rather than silently changing evidence labels.
  5. Review scientific plausibility, source limitations, accessibility, and reuse rights. Publish accepted changes with a dated change log.

Keep a candidate queue

Download the candidate template (JSON) and read the review workflow. The template records ideas, evidence, limitations, verification dates, and editorial decisions. Save a separate file for each candidate using a stable ID.

The website itself does not search the web or save submissions. Periodic research runs belong in the project’s review workflow; accepted content is then added to the collection and published in a new edition.

What a map can—and cannot—show

Maps organize the relationships already recorded in the dataset. Sharing a source or a broad field family does not establish a direct link between two disciplines. A new cross-disciplinary edge needs its own explanation and review. This collection is a starting map, not proof that every conceivable discipline necessarily depends on geoscience.
